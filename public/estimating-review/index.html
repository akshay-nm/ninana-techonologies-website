Questions on MES Estimating Practice

We are writing down, figure by figure, how quantities are taken off and resources estimated for MES work under the SSR: the bill of quantities, the bar bending schedule, the resource bill and what follows from them. Where the SSR speaks we have read it; where it is silent we have made an assumption. Before we rely on any of it, we would like an experienced hand to look at the 23 points below.

Each point says what we do now, what the SSR says, what else could be done, and what the choice moves on a worked example: a single-storey RCC frame of three bays by one, with footings on plain concrete beds, plinth beams, columns, roof beams and a roof slab with openings.

MES SSR Part II 2020

Measuring the work

How quantities are taken off. Every rule here changes a figure in the bill of quantities.

1

Steel billed in three bands

From the SSR
What we do now
Bars of 10 mm and over; bars of 6 to 8 mm; stirrups, spacers and binders up to 10 mm. A bar goes to the first band that fits.
What the book says
Section 10 of Part II prices the three apart.
Alternatives
Bind by role first (a binder is a binder at any size) or by size first.
What it moves on the example
The largest mover on the example: moving the 6 to 8 mm band up one size puts 307 kg into it, up 197 in a hundred, and takes 19 in a hundred from the 10 mm and over item.

The question. Is an 8 mm main bar in a slab billed with the 6 to 8 mm item, and an 8 mm stirrup with the binders item?

Ruling
2

Excavation for a footing: no working space, lifts of 1.5 m

Our assumption
What we do now
The pit is the plan of the bed, widened by nothing, times the depth from ground to the underside of the bed. The depth is paid in lifts of 1.5 m. A dig whose narrower side is not over 1.5 m is a trench whatever its length; not over 10 sqm on plan is a pit; anything larger is excavation over areas.
What the book says
Part II items 03004 to 03007 and clauses 3.1.1 and 3.1.9. Part I has no clause on working space for pits (only 5.43, for wells).
Alternatives
A working space on each side (30 cm is common); a trench judged by its length as well as its width.
What it moves on the example
30 cm of working space each side takes the example from 34.68 to 63.48 cum of excavation, up 83 in a hundred.

The question. Is 0 the MES rule for footings, or does the tender have to say so each time?

Ruling
3

Surplus earth removed does not swell

Our assumption
What we do now
Surplus is the dug volume less the backfill, with a bulking factor of 1.0. Backfill is item 03009 with a 10 per cent deduction (5 for heavy soil) under clause 3.6.
What the book says
Part II clause 3.6 and note (f) to item 03009 give the deduction. Neither part gives a bulking factor for surplus.
Alternatives
A factor of 1.25 for ordinary soil, as many estimators take.
What it moves on the example
At 1.25 the removal item goes from 11.76 to 14.70 cum, up 25 in a hundred.

The question. Does MES pay removal on the net dug volume or on a bulked volume?

Ruling
4

Slab edges under 20 cm by the running metre

From the SSR
What we do now
An edge not over 20 cm high is measured by its length under items 07021 to 07023 by height band; only an edge over 20 cm is measured in square metres under 07008.
What the book says
Part II items 07008 and 07021 to 07023, lines 6592 to 6711.
Alternatives
Perimeter times thickness in square metres, as we did before.
What it moves on the example
The the example roof slab edge went from 5.26 sqm to 42.10 rm under 07021.

The question. Confirm the reading, and that the edge of an opening in the slab is measured the same way.

Ruling
5

Each dimension is rounded before the quantity

From the SSR
What we do now
Lengths to 0.01 m, thicknesses and sections to 0.005 m, before multiplying. Bar lengths to 5 mm. Steel to the whole kilogram on the bill item.
What the book says
Part II general rules 1.9 and Section 10 clauses 10.1.2 and 10.1.3.
Alternatives
Compute in full and round the quantity once at the end.
What it moves on the example
Steel 10 mm and over: 1586.35 kg before the rounding, 1586 kg after. Small on the example; larger on a long slab.

The question. Confirm the steps, and whether the rounding is half-up.

Ruling
6

Openings deducted above 0.1 sqm in concrete and 0.4 sqm in formwork

Open
What we do now
Written from memory of IS 1200 Parts 2 and 5.
What the book says
Neither part of the SSR read gives these thresholds.
Alternatives
Deduct every opening; or the SSR general rule, if one exists that we did not find.
What it moves on the example
On the example the roof openings are all above both limits, so nothing moves; on a slab with many small sleeves it would.

The question. What thresholds does MES apply?

MES SSR Part I 2009, Section 10

Detailing the steel

What goes into the bar bending schedule. Part I now gives most of it; the contract overrides all of it.

7

Lap length: the greater of the development length and 30 diameters

Open
What we do now
Every lap is taken as in flexural tension: the development length or 30 d, whichever is greater. The development length is held at 50 d. Where the tender sets a lap (the example says 45 d) it stands.
What the book says
Part I clause 10.19.2: tension, development length per IS 456 clause 26.2.1 or 30 d, the greater; compression, 24 d. IS 456 itself has not been read.
Alternatives
Work the development length from IS 456 by grade of steel and concrete; or a flat 50 d; or keep the tender figure only.
What it moves on the example
Nothing on the example today, since the tender’s 45 d stands.

The question. For Fe500 in M25, what development length does MES accept, and does a tender’s 45 d clear it?

Ruling
8

Deformed bars have no hooks; binders keep theirs

From the SSR
What we do now
A deformed main bar with hooks is refused; binders, stirrups and links take a hook with a straight of at least 8 d beyond the curve. The hook lengths (8, 10 and 16 d for 90, 135 and 180 degrees) are our figures from IS 2502.
What the book says
Part I clause 10.18: deformed bars are bent to a radius of 4 d with 4 d straight beyond; their ends are not bent to form hooks.
Alternatives
Drop hooks on all bars; or keep hooks on all bars as IS 2502 shapes.
What it moves on the example
A 135 degree hook at 12 d instead of 10 d moves the binders item from 418 to 428 kg, up 2.4 in a hundred.

The question. Confirm, and give the hook length MES takes for stirrups.

Ruling
9

Bend deduction at a 4 d radius

Open
What we do now
Deductions of 1, 2, 3 and 4 diameters for bends of 45, 90, 135 and 180 degrees, from common practice on IS 2502. Part I’s radius of 4 d for deformed bars is held but the deductions were not reworked for it.
What the book says
Part I clause 10.18 gives the radius and no deduction.
Alternatives
Rework the deductions by geometry at 4 d; keep the common figures; deduct nothing.
What it moves on the example
One diameter more on a 135 degree bend takes the binders item from 418 to 413 kg, down 1.2 in a hundred.

The question. Which deductions does MES accept on a bar bending schedule?

Ruling
10

End cover at bar ends

Open
What we do now
Bars run to the concrete face less the side cover; no end cover is taken off at bar ends.
What the book says
Part I clause 4.11.8.1 gives cover by member; we hold it for the record. We keep the tender’s covers.
Alternatives
Take 25 mm or 2 d off each bar end.
What it moves on the example
Would shorten the example’s slab, beam and column bars by 10 to 14 mm each.

The question. Should end cover come off the cutting length?

Ruling
11

Unit mass of bars

From the SSR
What we do now
IS 1786 nominal masses, written from memory: 0.395 kg/m for 8 mm, 0.617 for 10, 0.888 for 12, 1.58 for 16.
What the book says
Part I names IS 1786 (clause 10.17.2) and gives no mass per metre, only the tolerance on weight (10.17.5).
Alternatives
None; the figures need checking against the published table.
What it moves on the example
A tenth more on 16 mm bars moves the 10 mm and over item from 1586 to 1676 kg, up 5.7 in a hundred.

The question. Check the four figures.

Ruling
12

Bar shapes reduced to four

From the SSR
What we do now
Straight, L, U and cranked; stirrups closed with two 135 degree hooks. A hooked end takes both the hook length and the bend deduction.
What the book says
IS 2502 shape codes; the SSR does not detail bars.
Alternatives
A hooked end takes the hook length only.
What it moves on the example
Up to 6.5 in a hundred on the binders item.

The question. Does a hooked end take a bend deduction as well as the hook length?

Part I where it speaks; CPWD DAR 2023 standing in

What one unit consumes

The figures behind the resource bill, the material requirement and the labour histogram. MES does not use the DAR; its figures stand only where no MES source and no firm figure exists.

13

Cement in M25 design mix: 330 kg, with Part I’s 300 kg as a floor

Our assumption
What we do now
6.6 bags (330 kg) a cum, the DAR’s figure for its own M25 analysis. Part I’s Table 7 minimum for reinforced concrete of M25 (300 kg in mild and moderate exposure) is held as a floor: a figure is raised to it only if it falls below.
What the book says
Part I clause 4.11.2, Table 7: minimum cement content by exposure and grade. It sets a minimum, not a consumption.
Alternatives
Take the 300 kg minimum as the consumption (6.0 bags); or leave cement blank until the mix design is approved.
What it moves on the example
At 6.0 bags the example’s cement falls from 207.09 to 188.99 bags.

The question. For estimating before the mix design, what cement content does MES practice take for M25?

PCC 1:4:8 materials from the DAR

Open
What we do now
3.4 bags cement, 0.47 cum sand, 0.65 cum of 40 mm and 0.24 cum of 20 mm aggregate a cum, from DAR analysis 4.1.8.
What the book says
Part I clause 4.11.2.4 gives the proportions by volume and the bag as the unit, but no dry volume to a cum and no split of coarse aggregate. It names IS 1911 for unit weights, which we have not read.
Alternatives
A dry volume of 1.52 with the proportions; the firm’s own yield.
What it moves on the example
the example has 8.1 cum of bed concrete.

16

Labour and machine days from the DAR, until the firm gives its own

Our assumption
What we do now
Every labour and machine figure is the DAR’s: excavation by machine at 0.006 mazdoor days a cum with an excavator and loader at 0.012 days; steel forms put up by a fitter (0.075 to 0.22 days a sqm); reinforcement at 0.01 blacksmith and 0.01 mazdoor days a kg. Each figure is marked as it is a CPWD figure standing in.
What the book says
Neither part of the SSR gives labour or machine days.
Alternatives
The firm’s own norms; the tender’s, where it gives any; manual excavation (we had 0.62 mazdoor days a cum).
What it moves on the example
Mazdoor on the example: 191.56 days before, 170.51 after; carpenter 68.64 to 21.04 days; a fitter of 37.36 days appears.

The question. Give the firm’s norms for these eight figures, or say which source MES estimators use.

Ruling
17

Shuttering and binding wire kept at our figures

Our assumption
What we do now
Shuttering 1 sqm-use to the sqm of contact area (0.2 to the running metre of edge); binding wire 0.009 kg to the kg of steel.
What the book says
Part I clause 10.20 specifies the wire (annealed, not under 0.9 mm) and gives no quantity. The DAR prices both as money only.
Alternatives
Drop both from the material list; take the firm’s figures.
What it moves on the example
Example: 227.38 sqm-use of shuttering, 19.44 kg of wire.

The question. Keep both on the material list? At what rate?

MES SSR Part I 2009, Section 4

Time, tests and pours

What moves the dates, the pour plan and the inspection and test plan, not the bill.

19

Stripping times from Table 10

From the SSR
What we do now
Vertical faces 2 days; slab soffits 3 with props left; beam soffits 7 with props left; props under slabs 7 days to 4.5 m span and 14 over; under beams 14 to 6 m and 21 over. Ordinary Portland cement, normal weather.
What the book says
Part I clause 4.11.6.3, Table 10, checked against the page. Table 12 gives shorter times at 16 degrees C; we do not use it.
Alternatives
The 1 day for vertical faces we had from IS 456.
What it moves on the example
One day more on every column and wall pour in the programme.

The question. Confirm Table 10 as the rule for the programme.

Ruling
20

Concrete sampling: IS 456 bands, with Part I’s waiver and limit held

Open
What we do now
Samples by the IS 456 table (1 to 5 cum one sample, 6 to 15 two, 16 to 30 three, 31 to 50 four, then one more per 50). Part I’s waiver below 15 cum and its 60 cum limit are held as figures and not yet applied.
What the book says
Part I clause 4.11.17: three specimens to a sample, tested at 28 days; the frequency by quantity.
Alternatives
Apply the waiver; treat the 60 cum as a floor on frequency.
What it moves on the example
The inspection and test plan’s cube count.

The question. How are the 15 cum waiver and the 60 cum limit read on an MES job?

22

Beams and slab cast together

Our assumption
What we do now
The roof beams and slab of a storey are one pour group; columns, footings and beds are their own.
What the book says
A site convention; no book speaks.
Alternatives
Beams first, slab after.
What it moves on the example
The pour breakdown and the dates of the planned progress curve.
