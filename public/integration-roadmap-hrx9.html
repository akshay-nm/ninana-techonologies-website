Eleven portals.
One missing layer.

MES has digitised the office record — monitoring, assets, enlistment, estimates, measurement, budget, arbitration, complaints. Each portal is strong on its slice. But the site itself — where every one of those records is born — still runs on paper. That is why the portals cannot talk to each other: there is nothing underneath them generating shared ground truth. Setu is that layer. Nothing gets replaced; everything gets connected.

The gap

Why the portals can't talk today

Each portal holds a record that is typed in after the fact — progress typed into WBPMP, measurements into EMB, evidence assembled for MIMAMSA when the dispute has already begun. The same event is re-entered three times by three appointments, and the versions never quite agree. Integration fails not for want of APIs, but because there is no single record of what actually happened at site.

Setu

Record it once, where it happens

Setu models MES procedure as it stands — offices, appointments, registers, verification chains. The contractor raises stage passing with photos and video; the diary is signed daily; materials are received, tested and reconciled; every action is timestamped and cannot be retracted. One record, born at site, feeding every portal above it. Nothing new to invent — it produces the artefacts MES procedure already requires.

The approach

No portal is replaced

Every portal remains the authority for its own record. Setu feeds them; the dashboard reads them. Integration starts read-only — data flows one way until each owner is satisfied — and grows portal by portal, exactly the way WBPMP–BMP-2 is already being attempted.

Feeds begin read-only — each portal keeps authority over its record Exchange formats agreed jointly with each portal's owners Phased: one GE, one work type first — then widen Setu runs on the establishment's own network, AI included The WBPMP–BMP-2 link already planned becomes the pattern, not the exception

Setu means bridge. This is what it bridges.
